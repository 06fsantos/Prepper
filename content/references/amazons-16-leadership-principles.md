The list a behavioral answer is scored against, one line each — a lookup you scan before a round,
not prose you compress. These are the explicit rubric at Amazon and the best first-party proxy for
the private ladders other companies grade against, which is why they are worth studying even when
you are not interviewing there ([Amazon: Leadership Principles](https://www.aboutamazon.com/about-us/leadership-principles)).
The round itself — why it is graded this way and how to answer — is
[[the-behavioral-round-proves-the-ladder|the behavioral round proves the ladder]]; the structure for
each story is [[the-star-method|STAR]].

## The 16, in order

| # | Principle | What it asks of you |
| - | --------- | ------------------- |
| 1 | **Customer Obsession** | Start from the customer and work backwards; earn and keep their trust rather than watch competitors. |
| 2 | **Ownership** | Think long-term, act on behalf of the whole company, never say "that's not my job." |
| 3 | **Invent and Simplify** | Seek new ideas from everywhere and find ways to simplify; accept being misunderstood for long periods. |
| 4 | **Are Right, A Lot** | Have strong judgment and good instincts; seek diverse views and work to disconfirm your own beliefs. |
| 5 | **Learn and Be Curious** | Never stop improving yourself; stay curious about new possibilities and explore them. |
| 6 | **Hire and Develop the Best** | Raise the bar with every hire and promotion; recognize talent and coach others, including moving them across the org. |
| 7 | **Insist on the Highest Standards** | Hold a relentlessly high bar — higher than many think reasonable — and drive quality up so defects don't travel downstream. |
| 8 | **Think Big** | Create and communicate a bold direction that inspires results; look around corners for ways to serve customers. |
| 9 | **Bias for Action** | Value calculated speed; many decisions are reversible ("two-way doors") and don't need heavy study. |
| 10 | **Frugality** | Accomplish more with less; constraints breed resourcefulness, self-sufficiency, and invention. |
| 11 | **Earn Trust** | Listen attentively, speak candidly, treat others respectfully, and stay self-critical even when it's awkward. |
| 12 | **Dive Deep** | Stay connected to the details, audit frequently, and be skeptical when metrics and anecdote disagree. |
| 13 | **Have Backbone; Disagree and Commit** | Challenge decisions respectfully when you disagree — then commit fully once the call is made, even if you lost. |
| 14 | **Deliver Results** | Focus on the key inputs, hit them with quality and on time, and rise to setbacks rather than settle. |
| 15 | **Strive to be Earth's Best Employer** | Build a safer, more productive, more equitable work environment and lead with empathy for people's growth. |
| 16 | **Success and Scale Bring Broad Responsibility** | Act with the humility that comes with scale; be better, do better, and leave things better than you found them. |

## What actually costs points

- **The senior weight is not spread evenly.** The principles that carry a senior answer are the
  ones that restate [[what-senior-means-as-a-level|the level]]: **Ownership** and **Bias for
  Action** (driving through ambiguity), **Hire and Develop the Best** (mentorship), and **Have
  Backbone; Disagree and Commit** (handling conflict — argue hard, then commit). A story that
  evidences these reads as senior; one that only shows you completed a task reads as mid-level.
- **Map stories to principles; do not memorize the list.** The recruiters' own advice is the
  inverse of what candidates do: prepare six to eight real stories and tag each with the
  principles it evidences, so you reach for a lived example rather than reciting a definition
  ([Amazon recruiter tips](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/workplace/recruiters-offer-their-best-tips-for-interviewing-at-amazon)).
  One good story usually covers several principles at once.
- **One story, several principles.** A led migration where the requirements were vague, you were
  overruled and committed anyway, and you can name the result in numbers hits Ownership, Bias for
  Action, and Have Backbone; Disagree and Commit in a single answer. That density is the target,
  not one rehearsed story per principle.
- **"Disagree and Commit" is one principle, not two moods.** The signal is the whole arc — you
  disagreed with data *and* committed fully once the decision went the other way. Telling only the
  disagreement reads as a complaint; telling only the commitment reads as having no backbone.

Worth reading in full: [Amazon's Leadership Principles](https://www.aboutamazon.com/about-us/leadership-principles)
— the canonical wording, which shifts over time (two principles were added in 2021), so treat this
table as the shape and the source as authoritative.
